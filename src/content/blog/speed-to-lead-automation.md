Someone fills in your enquiry form at 9pm. They've been thinking about this for a while, and tonight they finally did something about it.

If nobody sees it until 10am the next morning, they've had 13 hours to find someone else. And they probably filled in two or three other forms that same night.

The business that replies first doesn't always win. But it gets the first conversation, and the first chance to be the one that's easy to deal with.

You don't need someone on call all night to fix this. You need a few automations that run the moment the form comes in.

## The first minute

When a new lead arrives, three things should happen straight away.

**1. An immediate reply, by text and email.** Not a generic "we've received your enquiry". Something that sounds like a person, says what happens next, and gives them a way to act right now.

For most service businesses, that means a booking link. Something like:

> Hi Sarah, thanks for getting in touch about your kitchen renovation. You can pick a time for a quick call here: [link]. If you'd rather we call you, just reply with a good time.

Short, specific, and it gives them a next step while they're still interested.

**2. The lead lands in your pipeline.** With the right stage, the right tags, and where they came from. That way nobody has to copy details from an email into the CRM, and nobody forgets to.

**3. Your team is told.** In the place they actually look: a phone notification, a team chat, an SMS. Make it clear who owns it. "New lead" sent to five people often means nobody follows up, because everyone assumes someone else did.

## The next day

Plenty of people won't book from the first message. They meant to, then got distracted.

So the next layer is a reminder if they haven't booked within a day. Keep it light:

> Hi Sarah, just checking you got the link to book a call. Happy to work around your schedule.

Then one more a few days later. After that, move them to a slower nurture sequence or mark them as cold. Three messages is plenty for most businesses. More than that starts to feel like chasing.

The important bit: **these messages should stop the moment they book or reply.** Nothing undoes a good first impression faster than a "just checking in" text after they've already booked.

## What it isn't

This isn't about replacing the human part. The goal isn't a chatbot that pretends to be you.

It's about not losing people in the gap before you get to talk to them. The automation holds the door open. You still do the actual conversation.

## Setting it up

You can build this in most CRMs. I usually set it up in GoHighLevel, but HubSpot, Pipedrive and others can do the same thing. The structure is the same everywhere:

1. **Trigger:** form submitted
2. **Actions:** create or update the contact, add to the pipeline, send the SMS and email, notify the team
3. **Wait:** one day
4. **Condition:** have they booked? If yes, stop. If no, send the reminder.
5. **Wait:** a few more days, then the last reminder

Some practical things worth getting right:

- **Send from a real name and a number people can reply to.** Replies should reach a person, not disappear.
- **Respect quiet hours for SMS.** A text at 2am doesn't look keen. It looks careless. Most tools let you hold texts until morning while still sending the email straight away.
- **Use what they told you.** If the form asks what they need, use it in the reply. "About your kitchen renovation" reads very differently to "about your enquiry".
- **Check the opt-in rules where you operate.** SMS consent rules vary by country.

## Test it like a customer

Once it's set up, fill in your own form from your phone, late at night, the way a real lead would. Then check:

- How long did the first message take?
- Does it read like a person wrote it?
- Did the right person get notified?
- If you don't book, does the reminder arrive the next day?
- If you do book, do the reminders stop?

It takes an afternoon to build and ten minutes to test. After that, it runs every single time, including at 9pm on a Sunday.
