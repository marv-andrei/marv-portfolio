When a funnel isn't bringing in calls, the first thing most teams look at is the landing page. The headline gets rewritten. The button changes colour. Someone suggests a new hero image.

Sometimes that's the right call. But a lot of the time, the page is doing its job. People are filling in the form. They're just disappearing somewhere after that.

The page is the part everyone can see, so it gets all the attention. What happens after the click is invisible until you go looking for it.

## The part nobody tests

Here's what usually needs to happen in the few minutes after someone submits a booking form:

- the lead is created in your CRM, with the right source and tags
- they land in the right stage of your pipeline
- they get a confirmation by email (and often SMS)
- if they booked a time, a calendar invite goes out to both sides
- someone on your team is told about it
- if they didn't book a time, a follow-up starts

That's six separate things, often handled by three or four different tools that were connected at different times by different people. Any one of them can quietly stop working, and the landing page will look exactly the same.

## A 10-minute check you can do today

You don't need a tool for this. You need a phone, an email address you can check, and ten minutes.

**1. Submit a real test lead.** Use your own name with a "+test" email (like you+test@gmail.com) and your actual phone number. Fill it in on your phone, because that's how most of your visitors will.

**2. Check the thank-you page.** Does it confirm what just happened and what happens next? "Thanks!" on its own leaves people unsure whether it worked.

**3. Open your CRM.** Is the contact there? Is it in the stage you'd expect? Does it show where the lead came from? If you run ads, the source matters, because it's how you know which campaign is paying for itself.

**4. Check your inbox and phone.** Did the confirmation arrive? How long did it take? Does it come from a name people will recognise, or from a no-reply address that looks like spam?

**5. Check the calendar.** If you booked a time, is it on the calendar with the right time zone? Time zones catch people out more often than you'd think, especially with clients in another country.

**6. Check the team notification.** Who was told about the new lead, and where? An email to an inbox nobody reads doesn't count.

**7. Run the "didn't book" path.** Submit a second test lead, but stop before picking a time. Then wait. Does anything follow up? When?

Write down what you find as you go. Most people find at least one thing that surprises them.

## What usually breaks

From the funnels I've worked on, these are the ones that come up again and again:

**Forms connected to the wrong pipeline.** Someone duplicated a funnel for a new campaign and the form still points to the old one. Leads arrive, but in a place nobody is watching.

**Confirmations that never send.** An email domain wasn't verified, or an SMS number ran out of credit. The workflow looks fine in the builder. It just isn't delivering.

**Notifications to the wrong person.** The person who set it up left, changed roles, or set it to themselves "for now".

**No follow-up for people who don't book.** This is the most common one. A good chunk of people fill in the form but never pick a time. Without a follow-up, they're gone.

**Duplicate contacts.** The same person fills in two forms and ends up as two records, getting two sets of messages.

None of these are hard to fix. They're just easy to miss, because nothing on the landing page tells you they're broken.

## How often to check

Run the full test whenever something changes: a new campaign, a new form, a new team member, a change to your calendar. Otherwise, once a month is plenty.

If you have more than one funnel, keep a simple list of each form and where its leads should go. It makes the check much faster, and it's the first thing you'll want when something does go wrong.

## The point

A great landing page that drops leads into a void is just an expensive form. Before you spend more on traffic or redesign the page, make sure the people already raising their hand are getting a reply.

It's ten minutes. Submit a test lead and follow it all the way through.
