I use AI on almost every build now. It writes first drafts of code, gives me twenty headline options in a minute, and turns a spec sheet into product copy. It makes me a lot faster.

So this isn't a post about AI being bad at building pages. It's good at it. You can describe a store, and in an afternoon you'll have something that looks finished.

The problem is the gap between "looks finished" and "works". That gap is where the money goes.

## What usually isn't working underneath

When I check a store or landing page that was put together quickly, with AI or a template, these are the things that come up again and again:

- **Tracking.** The Meta pixel or Google tag is installed, but the purchase or lead event never fires. The page gets traffic, and the ad platform has no idea who bought, so it can't find more people like them.
- **Emails.** Order confirmations, shipping notices and abandoned cart emails still have the default template text, or aren't switched on at all.
- **Mobile.** Everything looks right on a laptop. On a phone, the discount field is hidden, a button is cut off, or the sticky bar covers the add-to-cart.
- **Shipping and returns.** Nothing about delivery times or returns until checkout, which is the worst moment for a surprise.
- **Forms.** The contact or booking form shows a nice "thanks" message, and the lead goes nowhere. No CRM, no email, no follow-up.
- **Speed.** Five apps added to do five small things, each loading its own script on every page.

None of these show up in a screenshot. They show up after you've spent money on ads and can't work out why nothing converted.

## AI answers the question you ask

This is the bigger issue, and it's not about code.

Ask AI to "make my product page convert better" and you'll get a stronger headline, some trust badges and a nicer layout. All reasonable.

But if the real problem is that your ad promises 50% off and the page doesn't mention it, none of that helps. If people are leaving because shipping costs appear at the last step, a new headline won't fix it. If leads are coming in at 9pm and nobody replies until morning, the page was never the problem.

AI doesn't know your margins, which ad someone clicked, what your customers complain about, or what happened the last time you tried something. It answers the question in front of it. Knowing which question to ask, and where to look first, comes from having done it before.

## It's also confidently wrong

AI will tell you something with total confidence that isn't true. On a build, that looks like:

- code that uses a Shopify feature that doesn't exist
- product copy promising free returns the store doesn't offer
- a translated button that's cut off on mobile because the word is twice as long
- a form that looks fixed but has stopped sending leads to the CRM

Everything looks fine until a real customer uses it. AI makes building faster. It doesn't make checking optional.

## When doing it yourself is fine

Honestly, plenty of the time.

If you're testing an idea, need a simple landing page, and aren't spending much on ads yet, build it with AI. It's cheap and fast, and you'll learn something.

It's worth getting someone who's done it before when:

- you're about to put real ad money behind the page
- leads need to land in a CRM and get followed up automatically
- you're selling in more than one country or currency
- checkout, tracking, emails and apps all have to work together
- the page is live and getting traffic, but not converting, and you don't know why

AI gets you a page. Those are the parts that decide whether the page makes money.

## What you're paying an expert for

If AI does half the building now, it's fair to ask what a developer is for.

Mostly it's the decisions. What goes above the fold. What to cut. Which app to drop and replace with a few lines of code. What breaks when you connect the CRM. What to test first. And what to check before launch, because that's where the expensive mistakes are.

The speed AI gives me goes to the client. They're still paying for the judgement.

## A quick check you can do today

If your store or page was built quickly, go through it like a customer would:

1. Open it on your phone, not your laptop.
2. Buy something, or submit the form, for real.
3. Check the confirmation email arrives and reads properly.
4. Check the lead or order shows up where it should: your CRM, your inbox, your store admin.
5. In your ad platform, check the purchase or lead event was recorded.

If all five work, you're in better shape than most. If one doesn't, that's what's costing you.
