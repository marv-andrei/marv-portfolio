Most stores have an abandoned cart email. Someone adds something to their cart, leaves, and an hour later gets "You left something behind!"

That's a good start. But it's usually the only email, and it's built on one assumption: that people left because they forgot.

Some did. Plenty didn't. Something made them hesitate, and a reminder doesn't answer that.

## Why people really leave

When someone gets as far as the cart and stops, it's usually one of these:

- **Price.** They wanted to think about it, or compare.
- **Shipping.** The cost or delivery time showed up at checkout and surprised them.
- **Sizing or fit.** They weren't sure they'd picked the right one.
- **Trust.** They've never bought from you and weren't sure about it.
- **Timing.** Something interrupted them. They meant to come back.

Only the last one is solved by "you left something behind". The others need an answer.

## The 3-email flow

Here's the setup I'd start with for most stores.

### Email 1: about an hour later. The reminder.

Keep it simple. Show the product with its photo, the name, the price, and a clear button back to the cart.

This catches the people who were interrupted. Don't add a discount here. Many of these people would have bought anyway, and training customers to wait for a discount gets expensive.

A subject line like "Still thinking it over?" or just the product name works fine. Clear beats clever.

### Email 2: the next day. Answer the doubt.

This is the email most stores are missing, and it's often the one that does the most work.

Instead of reminding them again, answer the questions that make people hesitate:

- **Reviews** from people who bought the same product
- **Sizing help,** or a link to the size guide
- **Returns,** explained simply: how long they have, and how easy it is
- **Delivery times** and costs, stated clearly

You don't need all of it. Pick what matters most for your product. For clothing, sizing and returns. For something expensive, reviews and guarantees. For a new brand, trust.

The tone should feel helpful, not pushy. You're making it easier for them to say yes, not chasing them.

### Email 3: a few days later. Last reminder.

One final email, with a reason to come back now.

If your margins allow, this is where a small incentive can go: free shipping or a modest discount. If they don't, a gentle note that stock is limited (only if it's true) or simply "your cart is still saved" is fine.

After this, stop. Three emails is enough. More than that starts to feel like being followed around.

## Things that make a big difference

**Stop the flow when they buy.** Make sure anyone who completes their order drops out of the sequence straight away. Getting "Don't forget your cart!" after you've already paid looks careless.

**Show the actual product.** Use dynamic content so the email shows what they left, not a generic "you left items in your cart". Most email tools for Shopify and other platforms can do this.

**Link straight back to the cart.** The button should restore their cart, not drop them on the homepage to find it again.

**Check it on a phone.** Most people will open these emails on their phone. Check the images load, the button is easy to tap, and the text isn't tiny.

**Look at email 2 separately.** When you review results, check each email on its own. If email 2 isn't getting clicks, the content probably isn't answering the right question for your customers.

## Setting it up

You can build this in most ecommerce email tools, like Klaviyo, Shopify Email or Omnisend. The structure is the same:

1. **Trigger:** checkout started (or added to cart), no order placed
2. **Wait:** about 1 hour, then send email 1
3. **Wait:** about 1 day, then send email 2
4. **Wait:** 2–3 more days, then send email 3
5. **Exit:** anyone who places an order leaves the flow

Then test it yourself. Add something to your cart, enter your email at checkout, leave, and make sure all three emails arrive, look right on your phone, and stop once you buy.

## The point

A reminder helps people who forgot. An answer helps people who hesitated. Most stores only send the reminder.
